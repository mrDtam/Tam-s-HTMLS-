Q1: State the reference angle for $250^\circ$.

Answer:

  • Step 1: Determine the quadrant. $250^\circ$ is between $180^\circ$ and $270^\circ$, so it is in Quadrant III.
  • Step 2: Use the formula for Quadrant III reference angle: $\theta_R = \theta - 180^\circ$.
  • Step 3: Calculate: $250^\circ - 180^\circ = 70^\circ$.
Q2: Determine one positive and one negative co-terminal angle for $135^\circ$.

Answer:

  • Step 1: To find a positive co-terminal angle, add $360^\circ$: $135^\circ + 360^\circ = 495^\circ$.
  • Step 2: To find a negative co-terminal angle, subtract $360^\circ$: $135^\circ - 360^\circ = -225^\circ$.

Q4: Identify the quadrant(s) where $\sin \theta < 0$ and $\tan \theta > 0$.

Answer:

  • Step 1: $\sin \theta < 0$ (sine is negative) in Quadrants III and IV.
  • Step 2: $\tan \theta > 0$ (tangent is positive) in Quadrants I and III.
  • Step 3: The overlapping quadrant satisfying both conditions is Quadrant III.
Q5: Find the area of $\triangle ABC$ where $a = 7$, $b = 10$, and $\angle C = 42^\circ$.

Answer:

  • Step 1: Use the area formula for a triangle given two sides and the contained angle: $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$.
  • Step 2: Substitute the values: $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(7)(10)\sin 42^\circ$.
  • Step 3: Calculate: $\text{Area} = 35 \times 0.6691 \approx 23.4$ square units.
Q6: Solve for side $x$ in $\triangle DEF$ using the Sine Law, given $\angle D = 40^\circ$, $d = 10$, $\angle E = 60^\circ$, and $e = x$.

Answer:

  • Step 1: Set up the Sine Law ratio: $\frac{x}{\sin E} = \frac{d}{\sin D}$.
  • Step 2: Substitute the known values: $\frac{x}{\sin 60^\circ} = \frac{10}{\sin 40^\circ}$.
  • Step 3: Isolate $x$: $x = \frac{10 \sin 60^\circ}{\sin 40^\circ}$.
  • Step 4: Calculate: $x = \frac{10(0.8660)}{0.6428} \approx 13.5$.
Q7: Solve for side $y$ in $\triangle GHI$ using the Cosine Law, given $g = 5$, $h = 8$, $\angle I = 110^\circ$, and $i = y$.

Answer:

  • Step 1: Use the Cosine Law formula for side $y$: $y^2 = g^2 + h^2 - 2gh\cos I$.
  • Step 2: Substitute values: $y^2 = 5^2 + 8^2 - 2(5)(8)\cos 110^\circ$.
  • Step 3: Simplify: $y^2 = 25 + 64 - 80(-0.3420) \approx 89 + 27.36 = 116.36$.
  • Step 4: Take the square root: $y = \sqrt{116.36} \approx 10.8$.
Q8: Evaluate the exact value of $\cos(300^\circ)$ without using a calculator.

Answer:

  • Step 1: Determine the reference angle. $300^\circ$ is in Quadrant IV. Reference angle $= 360^\circ - 300^\circ = 60^\circ$.
  • Step 2: Note the sign of cosine in Quadrant IV. Cosine is positive (CAST rule).
  • Step 3: Evaluate using special triangles: $\cos(300^\circ) = +\cos(60^\circ) = \frac{1}{2}$.
Q9: The point $P(-3, 4)$ lies on the terminal arm of angle $\theta$ in standard position. Determine the exact value of $\sec \theta$ and $\cot \theta$.

Answer:

  • Step 1: Identify $x = -3$ and $y = 4$.
  • Step 2: Find the radius $r$ using $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = \sqrt{(-3)^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9+16} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.
  • Step 3: Use the ratio for secant: $\sec \theta = \frac{r}{x} = \frac{5}{-3} = -\frac{5}{3}$.
  • Step 4: Use the ratio for cotangent: $\cot \theta = \frac{x}{y} = \frac{-3}{4} = -\frac{3}{4}$.
Q10: Solve $2\sin \theta + 1 = 0$ for $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$ without a calculator.

Answer:

  • Step 1: Isolate $\sin \theta$: $2\sin \theta = -1 \implies \sin \theta = -\frac{1}{2}$.
  • Step 2: Find the reference angle where $\sin \theta_R = \frac{1}{2}$. From special triangles, $\theta_R = 30^\circ$.
  • Step 3: Sine is negative in Quadrants III and IV.
  • Step 4: Quadrant III solution: $\theta = 180^\circ + 30^\circ = 210^\circ$.
  • Step 5: Quadrant IV solution: $\theta = 360^\circ - 30^\circ = 330^\circ$.
Q11: Solve $\cos^2 \theta - \frac{1}{2}\cos \theta = 0$ for $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$.

Answer:

  • Step 1: Factor the common term: $\cos \theta (\cos \theta - \frac{1}{2}) = 0$.
  • Step 2: Set each factor to zero: $\cos \theta = 0$ or $\cos \theta = \frac{1}{2}$.
  • Step 3: Solve $\cos \theta = 0$. The angles are $\theta = 90^\circ, 270^\circ$.
  • Step 4: Solve $\cos \theta = \frac{1}{2}$. The reference angle is $60^\circ$. Cosine is positive in Quadrants I and IV. $\theta = 60^\circ, 300^\circ$.
  • Step 5: Final solutions: $\theta = 60^\circ, 90^\circ, 270^\circ, 300^\circ$.
Q12: Solve $3\tan \theta - 5 = 0$ for $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$. Round to one decimal place.

Answer:

  • Step 1: Isolate $\tan \theta$: $3\tan \theta = 5 \implies \tan \theta = \frac{5}{3}$.
  • Step 2: Use a calculator to find the reference angle: $\theta_R = \tan^{-1}(\frac{5}{3}) \approx 59.0^\circ$.
  • Step 3: Tangent is positive in Quadrants I and III.
  • Step 4: Quadrant I solution: $\theta = 59.0^\circ$.
  • Step 5: Quadrant III solution: $\theta = 180^\circ + 59.0^\circ = 239.0^\circ$.
Q13: Two tracking stations are 30 km apart. Station A spots a satellite at an angle of elevation of $52^\circ$, and Station B spots the same satellite at an angle of elevation of $70^\circ$. If the satellite is between the two stations, how high is the satellite above the ground?

Answer:

  • Step 1: Draw a triangle with base 30 km and base angles $52^\circ$ (at A) and $70^\circ$ (at B).
  • Step 2: Find the third angle at the satellite (C): $\angle C = 180^\circ - 52^\circ - 70^\circ = 58^\circ$.
  • Step 3: Use the Sine Law to find side $b$ (distance from A to satellite): $\frac{b}{\sin 70^\circ} = \frac{30}{\sin 58^\circ} \implies b = \frac{30 \sin 70^\circ}{\sin 58^\circ} \approx 33.24$ km.
  • Step 4: Drop a perpendicular from C to form a right triangle to find height $h$: $\sin 52^\circ = \frac{h}{b}$.
  • Step 5: Calculate $h$: $h = 33.24 \sin 52^\circ \approx 26.2$ km.
Q14: A ship leaves port and travels 15 km on a bearing of $120^\circ$. It then changes course to a bearing of $200^\circ$ and travels 20 km. How far is the ship from the port?

Answer:

  • Step 1: Sketch the path. Vector 1 is 15 km at $120^\circ$. Vector 2 is 20 km at $200^\circ$.
  • Step 2: Find the interior angle of the triangle formed. The back bearing of the first leg is $120^\circ + 180^\circ = 300^\circ$. The new bearing is $200^\circ$. The angle between them is $300^\circ - 200^\circ = 100^\circ$.
  • Step 3: Use the Cosine Law to find the distance $d$: $d^2 = 15^2 + 20^2 - 2(15)(20)\cos 100^\circ$.
  • Step 4: Calculate: $d^2 = 225 + 400 - 600(-0.1736) = 625 + 104.16 = 729.16$.
  • Step 5: Final distance: $d = \sqrt{729.16} \approx 27.0$ km.
Q15: In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle A = 30^\circ$, $b = 10$, and $a = 6$. Determine how many distinct triangles can be formed, and find all possible values of $\angle B$.

Answer:

  • Step 1: Calculate the height of the triangle: $h = b \sin A = 10 \sin 30^\circ = 5$.
  • Step 2: Compare $h$, $a$, and $b$: Since $h (5) < a (6) < b (10)$, this is the ambiguous case and TWO distinct triangles can be formed.
  • Step 3: Use the Sine Law to find the primary angle $B_1$: $\frac{\sin B_1}{10} = \frac{\sin 30^\circ}{6} \implies \sin B_1 = \frac{10(0.5)}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$.
  • Step 4: Calculate $B_1$: $\angle B_1 = \sin^{-1}(\frac{5}{6}) \approx 56.4^\circ$.
  • Step 5: Find the supplementary angle $B_2$ for the second triangle: $\angle B_2 = 180^\circ - 56.4^\circ = 123.6^\circ$.
